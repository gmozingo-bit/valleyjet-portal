Hi Kathy,

Great question, and you're not alone. Most people don't know these jets by name until they've flown a few. Here's what actually separates them, so you can pick the one that feels right.

Three short hops, Phoenix area to Moab, Moab to Grants, and Grants back home, each about an hour to an hour and a half in the air, all nonstop with two pilots up front. Lavatory setups differ between these aircraft, so I've noted each one below.

The three things that really differ

Cabin heightWhether it's a walk-around cabin or a seated cabin you settle into, like first class.
Cabin widthHow much elbow and leg room there is between the facing seats.
Bag spaceHow much luggage fits, and whether you can reach your bags during the flight.

How the cabins feel

You're in the air about an hour and a half, and you'll be seated for nearly all of it, just like a first-class seat. Height mostly matters for the walk to the lav.

My take

If you want room to get up and walk around, it's the Challenger 300, and it's priced right with the light jets.

If value matters most, the Citation CJ4 is the lowest price on the list and still seats all five with an enclosed lavatory.

If you'd like a turboprop for the regional fields, the PC-12 or the King Air 350i, with the King Air the roomier of the two.
