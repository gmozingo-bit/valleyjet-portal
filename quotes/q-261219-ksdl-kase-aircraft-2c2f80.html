Hi David,

Most people don't know these aircraft by name until they've flown a few. Here's what actually separates them, so you can pick the one that feels right.

All of these fly the 414 nautical miles nonstop, about 1 hour 20 minutes to 1 hour 40 minutes in the jets and about 2 hours in the turboprops. Lavatory setups differ between these aircraft, so I've noted each one below.

The three things that really differ

Cabin heightWhether it's a walk-around cabin or a seated cabin you settle into, like first class.
Cabin widthHow much elbow and leg room there is between the facing seats.
Bag spaceHow much luggage fits, and whether you can reach your bags during the flight.

How the cabins feel

You're in the air about an hour and a half, and you'll be seated for nearly all of it, just like a first-class seat. Height mostly matters for the walk to the lav.

Wi-Fi varies from jet to jet, even within the same model, so I don't want to promise it on a type. Once you narrow it to one or two, I'll confirm whether that specific aircraft has it.

My take

If price matters most, the Premier I is the lowest-priced jet and quick to Aspen.

If you want room to get up and walk around the cabin a little, look at the Learjet 60XR, which was redone in 2025 and is an easy step up for the money.

If bags are the main concern, a PC-12 makes loading easy, and the Sovereign+ has the most bag room of the jets.

The aircraft you fly will be the exact one pictured or one that's very similar in the fleet.
