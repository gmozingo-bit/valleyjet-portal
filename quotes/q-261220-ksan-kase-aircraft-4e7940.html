Hi Ali,

Great question, and you're not alone. Most people don't know these jets by name until they've flown a few. Here's what actually separates them, so you can pick the one that feels right.

It's about 640 nautical miles each way. The jets do it nonstop in roughly 2 to 2.5 hours and the turboprops in about 2.5 to 3, all with two pilots up front. One option makes a quick fuel stop on the way home. Lavatory setups differ between these aircraft, so I've noted each one below.

The three things that really differ

HeadroomWhether you can stand straight up or walk the aisle with a slight stoop.
Cabin widthHow much elbow and leg room there is between the facing seats.
Bag spaceHow much luggage fits, and whether you can reach your bags during the flight.

About Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi varies from jet to jet, even within the same model, so I don't want to promise it on a type. Once you narrow it to one or two, I'll confirm whether that specific aircraft has it.

My take

If price matters most, it's the Phenom 100. Nothing else comes close, as long as your bags fit.

If you want the best balance for four with ski gear, it's the Citation CJ3 or CJ4 out of Palomar.

If you liked the King Air last time, the King Air 350i is the roomiest turboprop here, with a long cabin and plenty of bag space.
